# Research Profile

Prefer claim-centered research. A result is not successful because a metric
improved; it is successful only when the claim under test survives its required
baselines, disproof conditions, and goal-relative tradeoffs.

Capability comes before mechanism. Establish whether the system can do the
thing, then open mechanism, boundary, simplification, and confound branches
when the result is strong, surprising, complex, story-critical, or taste-risky.

Necessity matters. A method that works but is matched by a simpler or stronger
baseline has not justified why it should be used. Current best-known methods
must be resolved through a baseline dossier, with naive and random/null
baselines as additional anchors.

Negative results should be learnable. Rejected hypotheses should produce
scoped one-line lessons when they can prevent repeated mistakes or redirect
future branch generation.

Do not collapse unresolved tradeoffs too early. If the root goal does not rank
performance over efficiency, simplicity, interpretability, or robustness, keep
parallel branches and prioritize by information gain per cost.
